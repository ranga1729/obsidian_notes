### Standards
![[Pasted image 20260524205835.png]]
The protocols and operation in upper OSI layers are used by software engineers and computer scientists. These protocols are defined by the Internet Engineering Task Force(IETF). 

The physical layer consist of electronic circuitry, media and connectors developed by engineers. So the physical layer's standards are governed by these organizations, 
- International Organization for Standardization(ISO)
- Telecommunication Industry Association/Electronic Industries Association(TIA/EIA)
- International Telecommunication Union(ITU)
- American National Standards Institute(ANSI)
- Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers(IEEE)
- National Telecommunication Regulatory Authority Including the Federal Communication Commission (FCC) in the USA and the European Telecommunication Standards Institute(ETSI)


Physical layer standards address three functional areas, 
- Physical Components
- Encoding
- Signaling

Physical components, 
- NIC
- Interfaces and connectors
- Cable materials
- Cable designs

### Signaling
Signaling is the method how the bit values "1" and "0" are represented on the physical medium. 
The method of signaling will vary based on the type of medium being used.
Ex: 
1. Copper cable - electrical signals

2. Fiber Optic - light pulses

### Bandwidth
Bandwidth is the capacity at which a medium can carry data. 
Digital bandwidth measures the amount of data that can flow from one place to another in a given amount of time.
Typically measured in **"kilobits per second"**(kbps), **"megabits per seconds"**(mbps), **"gigabits per second"**(gbps). 
![[Pasted image 20260524223927.png]]

Bandwidth is not a measurement of speed. 
Ex: both 10Mbps and 100Mbps  Ethernet, bits are sent at the speed of electricity. 
The difference is the number of bits that are transmitted per second.

Bandwidth depend on,
- Properties of the physical media
- Technologies used in the networking

Latency : Amount of time, including delays for data to travel from one given point to another. 
Throughput: 
	The measure of the transfer of bits across the media over a given period of time. 
	Throughput is not equal to bandwidth and its always less than bandwidth. 
	Factors influence throughput,
	- Amount of network traffic
	- Type of traffic
	- Latency
Goodput: 
	The measure of usable data transfer over a given period of time. 
	Goodput = throughput - traffic overhead for establishing sessions, acknowledgments, encapsulation and transmitted bits. 

**Bandwidth is the maximum theoretical capacity of a network to transfer data.** 
**Throughput is the actual amount of data successfully transferred over a specific period.**
