The most common cable type.
Inexpensive, easy to install, has low resistant to current flow. 

Limitations:
1. Attenuation - The longer the electrical signals have to travel, the weaker they get. 
2. Distortion and corruption cause by external factors. 

External interferences, 
- Electromagnetic Interference(EMI) or Radio Frequency Interference(RFI)
- Crosstalk - Disturbance caused by the electric or magnetic fields of a signal on one wire to the signal in an adjacent wire. 
![[Pasted image 20260524234017.png]]

Mitigations:
- Strict adherence to the cable length limits to mitigate attenuation.
- Use a metallic shield to cover cable to mitigate EMI and RFI interferences. 
- Twist adjacent cable pairs to mitigate crosstalk.

### Unshielded Twisted Pair(UTP)
![[Pasted image 20260525005842.png]]
- Terminated with RJ45 connecters. 
- Interconnect hosts with intermediary network devices. 

Characteristics,
1. The outer jacket protects the copper wires from physical damage. 
2. Twisted pairs protect signal from interference.
3. Color-coded plastic insulation electrically isolates the wires from each other. 
### Shielded Twisted Pair(STP)
![[Pasted image 20260525010321.png]]
- Better noise protection. 
- More expensive than UTP. 
- Harder to install than UTP.
- Terminated with RJ45 cables.
- Interconnects hosts with intermediary network devices. 

characteristics,
1. The outer jacket protect the cable from physical damage. 
2. Braided or foil shield provides EMI/RFI protection.
3. Foil shield for each pair of wires provides EMI/RFI protection.
4. Color-coded plastic insulation electrically isolates the wires from each other and identifies each pair. 
### Co-Axial Cable
![[Pasted image 20260525010810.png]]
Consist of,
- Outer cable jacket to prevent physical damage
- A woven copper braid, or metallic foil, acts as the second wire in the circuit and as a shield for the inner conductor.
- A layer of flexible plastic insulation
- A copper conductor is used to transmit the electronic signals.
