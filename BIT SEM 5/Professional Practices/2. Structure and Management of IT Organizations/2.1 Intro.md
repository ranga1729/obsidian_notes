	ex: School and Colleges, Hospitals

- Types of commercial organizations
	1. Sole traders
	2. Partnerships
	3. Cooperatives
	4. Limited companies
#### 1. Sole traders
- An individual who runs their own business.
- No legal formalities to become a one. 
- Sole trader is personally liable for all the debts/assets.
- Best practice to register the business as a limited company. 
#### 2. Partnerships
- A group of people carrying on a business
- In a partnership,
	- Liability of partners is unlimited.
	- Partners are jointly and severally responsible for the partnership's liability.
- Often common in law, medicine, architecture fields. 
- Difficult to make changes in the ownership.  
#### 3. Cooperatives
- User-owned and user-controlled businesses.
- Often common in agriculture. 
#### 4. Limited companies
- In an unlimited company, the shareholders are personally liable for all the company's debt. 
- A limited company is limited by shares of by guarantee.
- Fundamental principles,
	1. The company is legally a person. 
	2. The ownership of the company is divided into a number of shares. 
	3. Owners of the company are not liable for debts/other liabilities of the company. Shareholders can only lose up to the amount they invested.

### Constitution of an Organization
- A company must have a written constitution in order to be registered. 
- Constitution consist of,
	1. The memorandum : Control its external relations
	2. Articles of association - How its internal affairs to be run
- Additionally, there may be a **shareholders' agreement**.
### Directors of a Company
- Directors are elected by the shareholders
- In small companies : shareholder are the directors or shareholder's close contacts 
- In large public companies : shareholders have very little opportunity to influence the directors
### Structure of a Company
- Organization structure states that how work is shared and how tasks and people are grouped together in an organization. Vary from org to org.
- Functional units of an organization,
	1. Production/operations
	2. Quality management
	3. Sales and marketing
	4. Finance and administration
	5. research and Development
- Depts of an organization :
	Number of layers in the structure. 
	Depends on the number of people reporting directly to a manager. (manager's span of control)
	![[Pasted image 20251219144943.png]]
- Centralization : 
	- In centralized companies - more power is kept at the top of the structure with delegation only when essential. Common in large manufacturing companies and long-established companies. 
	- In decentralized companies - more power is delegated to the lowest level. Common in high-tech companies where talents are at the bottom layer. 
	- Flexible centralization - rules and practices are laid down but overriding them is accepted in specific cases.
