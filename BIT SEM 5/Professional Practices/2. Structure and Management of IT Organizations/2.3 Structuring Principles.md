- Ways of grouping tasks and activities in an organization. 
- Different parts of the org may use different structuring principles. 
- Can be based on,
	1. Function
	2. Geography
	3. Product line
	4. Market sector
	5. Technology

#### Geography Based Structure
![[Pasted image 20251219153612.png]]
- Common in multinational companies that operate in a number of different countries.
- A company may be legally registered in a country as a subsidiary but owned by the parent company. 
- Subsidiaries are subject to the laws of the countries in which they are registered. 
#### Product Line Structure
- Based around the different types of product that an organization produces. 
Ex: A company that produces and market software
	Main operational division: development and maintenance, consultancy, training
#### Structured by Market Sector 
- Based on the different market sectors to which its customers or prospective customers belong.
- Common in software industry. 
- Advantage
	1. Both sales and technical staff are likely to be familiar with customers problems and each division can identify potential customers. 
- Disadvantage 
	1. one division may be unaware of technological expertise that exists in another division. It will lead to inefficient use of resources through unnecessarily hiring additional specialists.
	2. Since concentrating on its traditional areas, company will miss new opportunities and will stagnate. 
#### Structure by Technology
- Not sufficiently 'customer-focused'
- Based on the technologies they sell rather than what customer needs. 
- Problems,
	1. Several different technologies may require to solve a customer's issue.
	2. Applications cannot be said to require specific technologies.
	3. Many competent employees having expertise which runs across a number of technologies.
	4. Difficult for sales and marketing staff to predict which potential clients will need which technology
