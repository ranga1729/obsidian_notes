
> [!NOTE] Professional Employees
> Professional employees are employees of a certain status, who are expected, within limits, to put the interests of the organization they work for above their own convenience. 

- To describe someone as a professional implies that they can be relied on to carry out their work competently and conscientiously regardless of the circumstances. 
- A professional piece of work means a piece of work that meets established standards of quality. 

Common characteristics of a professions,
1. Substantial education and training required.
2. The members of the profession decide the nature of this training and entry to the profession. 
3. The profession is organized into one or more professional bodies. 
4. Members of the profession are expected to conduct their professional activities in accordance with codes of conduct laid down by the professional bodies.
##### Professional Bodies
Start by a group of people coming together because of a shared interest. 
Ex:
UK
1. BSC - The charted Institute for IT(BCS).
	Setup in 1957 as the "British Computer Society" by a group of people working in the IT. 
2. IET - The Institute of Engineering and Technology
	Formed in 2006.
US
3. IEEE - The Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineering
	The first professional society in the field of IT founded in 1946. 
4. ACM - Association of Computing Machinery
	Founded in 1947. 
##### Royal Charters
![[Pasted image 20250823132439.png]]
Royal charter is a formal grant issues by a monarch(king/queen) that gives legal status, rights or powers to an individual, organization, city or institute. 
It's basically an official document, sealed with the royal seal, that defines the existence of a body and sets out what it can do. 

Ex: 
1. British East India Company - 1600)
2. BCS - 1984
3. IET(after the merger) - 2008
4. BBC, University of Cambridge, The Institute of Charted Accountants 

A charted body often gets special recognition, independence from government control and authority to set standards.
Ex: who can call themselves a "Charted Accountant, Charted Engineer".
Some former British colonies (like Canada, India, Sri Lanka, etc.) still have institutions founded by Royal Charter during colonial times.

Royal charted institutes are authorized to do,
- Establish a code of conduct
- Promoting education on that field.
- Setting standards of education
- Establish mechanism of spreading the knowledge of good practices and new developments.(through publications and conferences)
- Promote standards and code of practices.
- Advice government and regulatory bodies about matter within its area.
##### Professional Conduct
- **Code of Conduct** → A set of rules, principles, and expected behaviors that guide how individuals should act in an organization or profession. It’s about ethics, values, and acceptable behavior
	Ex: honesty, respect, integrity
- **Code of Practice** → A set of detailed instructions, standards, and procedures that explain how tasks or activities should be carried out in line with laws or professional standards. It’s about practical implementation. 
	Ex: safety procedures, technical standards, compliance steps

BCS's code of conduct is currently divided into the following sections,
1. The public interest
2. Professional competence and integrity
3. Duty of the relevant authority
4. Duty of the profession
