Organizing is the deployment of organizational resources to achieve strategic goals. 

Organizing involves three basic functions,
- Job designing
- Departmentalization
- Coordination(Vertical and horizontal)

#### 1. Job Designing
Determining tasks and responsibilities of a job. 
- Job simplification: Grouping limited and highly related tasks so the jobholder has only a small number of narrow activities to perform.
- Job rotation: Systematically shifting workers through similar jobs periodically. 
- Job enlargement: Broaden the scope of the job.(Horizontal loading)
- Job enrichment: Increase the number of tasks and control that the employee has over the tasks.(vertical loading)

#### 3.1 Vertical Coordination
Vertical coordination is the process of synchronizing activities and aligning goals across different levels of an organization's hierarchy, from top management to frontline employees. It involves both downward communication of instructions and upward communication of feedback to ensure everyone works together harmoniously to achieve the company's overall objectives.
![[Pasted image 20251106133619.png]]
#### 3.2 Horizontal Coordination
Horizontal coordination is the process of managing activities among individuals or departments at the same hierarchical level to achieve common goals. 
It involves collaboration and communication across teams to ensure consistent effort and prevent conflicting priorities. This type of coordination is crucial for tasks requiring input from different departments, like production and marketing, and can be achieved through mechanisms such as cross-functional teams.

### Basic Principles of Organizing
1. Span of control: A number of subordinates that a given superior can effectively control.
2. Chain of command: All the positions in an organization should be linked to the top position of the organization through the successive layers. 
3. Unity of command: Each person within an organization must have only one clear reporting relationship to only one boss.
4. Scalar principle: A clear and unbroken line of authority that extends from the lowest to the highest position in the organization.
5. Delegation: Act of assigning a portion of managers job to the immediate lower level through granting authority , responsibility.
6. Authority: Refers to legitimate power that exists to exert influence within a certain boundary(right to make decision, carry out actions and direct others).
7. Line authority: The authority exercised by positions which are directly responsible purpose/major goals.
8. Staff authority: The authority assigned to the positions who are not directly responsible for the basic purpose/major goals of an organizations and play mainly advisory role to line positions within the organization.
9. Responsibility: The obligation to carry out duties and achieve goals related to the job.
10. Accountability: requirement to provide satisfactory reasons for significant deviations from duties or expected results.
11. Decentralization: bringing the authority from the top to the possible lowest level
12. Empowerment: Giving the certain authority to operational employees over their tasks with the sufficient training and resource to exercise the authority given.
