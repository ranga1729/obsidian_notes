### Intro
![[Pasted image 20250512112634.png]]
Extreme Programming (XP) is an Agile software development methodology that focuses on improving software quality and responsiveness to changing customer requirements. It emphasizes:
- Frequent releases in short development cycles
- Continuous feedback from the customer
- Test-driven development (TDD)
- Pair programming
- Simple design and refactoring
XP promotes close collaboration, constant communication, and technical excellence to ensure the software evolves with high quality.

Short timeboxed iterations provide structure for the XP. 
Team produces potentially shippable software at the end of each iteration. 
Each iteration start with a brief planning session and end with a product demo and retrospective. 
![[Pasted image 20250512114046.png]]

XP can eliminates, 
- Requirements
- Design
- Testing phases
- Formal documents

XP team produces deployable software every week by working it iterations. 
(week-long increments of work)
![[Pasted image 20250512120220.png]]
Every week the team does it a bit of,
- Release Planning
- Design
- Coding
- Testing
Team work on stories(small features)
Every week, the team commit to delivering 4 - 10 stories. 
Throughout the week, they work on all phases of development for each story.
At the end of the week, they deploy their software for internal review.
##### Planning

Customers:
	Responsible for making business decisions

	creating stories
	constructing a release plan
	Reviews
	Managing risks.
Programmers + Team
	Provide estimates and suggestions

#### Analysis
Customers
	Sit with the team full-time
	Determine requirements
	Answer programmers' questions
Testers + Customers
	Create the customer tests for a story around the same time that programmers implement the story.
#### Design and coding
- XP continuously create incremental design and architecture improvements.
- Follow Test-Driven Development(TDD)
Programmers
	Maintain coding standards
	share ownership of the code
	Fix issues
	Ensure code is deployable.
#### Testing
- TDD produce automated unit and integration tests. 
- Customers review work in progress to ensure that the UI works the way they expect.
- Testers explore the nonfunctional characteristics, such as performance and stability.
#### Deployment
- Team ready to keep the code deployable.
- Deployment to real customers is scheduled according to business needs.

### On-site Customers
In Extreme Programming (XP), on-site customers are real users or business representatives who work closely with the development team on a daily basis.
They Define the software that team builds. 
Release planning is their most important activity. 
Other responsibilities,
	Identify features/stories.
	Group features,
	frequent releases, 
	Manage risks,
	Create an achievable plan by coordinating with programmers
They refining their plans by soliciting feedback from real customers and other stakeholders.
Responsible for providing programmers with requirements details upon request.

Who play the On-site customers role ?
1. Product managers
2. Domain experts
3. Interaction designers
4. Business analysts
#### Programmers
Responsible for finding the most effective way of delivering the stories in the plan. Spend most of their time in pair programming. 
Using test-driven development, they write tests, implement code, refactor, and incrementally design and architect the application.
Have to have the awareness of technical debt and its impact on development time and future maintenance costs.
#### Testers
- Provide information about the software’s nonfunctional characteristics, such as performance, scalability, and stability, by using both exploratory testing and long-running automated tests.
- XP automates repetitive testing rather than performing manual regression testing.
#### Coaches
- Helping the team reach its potential.
- Leadership roles dynamically switch from person to person as situations dictate.
- They help set up conditions for energized work, and they assist the team in creating an informative workspace.
- Take responsibility for any reporting needed.
#### Other team members
Ex: 
	Technical Writers
	Analyst

- Interaction designers focuses on understanding users, their needs, and how they will interact with the product. They interview users, create user personas, review paper prototypes with users, and observing usage of actual software.
- Business analysts clarify and refine customer needs. 
- Designers and architects guide the incremental design and architecture efforts.

#### Filling roles
One product manager and programmer coach is needed at minimum.
Other roles can be blend together.
Ex: 
	Product managers can full project manager's role
	One customer can be the interaction designer.

- For new teams, 4-6 programmers is a good starting point. 
- XP teams can be as small as one experienced programmer and one product manager.
- The smallest team with full XP consists of five people: 
	four programmers (one acting as coach)and one product manager (who also acts as project manager, domain expert, and tester)
