Sprint planning is used to determine the most important subset of product backlog items to build in the next sprint. 
The combination of prioritized backlog items and the plan form the sprint backlog. 

Sprint planning participants,
(Full scrum team)
- Product owner
	Shares the initial sprint goal.
	Presents the prioritized product backlog.
	Answer questions
- The development team
	Determine what can be delivered within the sprint
- Scrum master
	Acts as the scrum team coach.
	Observe the planning
	Asks questions and facilitate the meeting

#### Sprint planning process
- Inputs,
	Product backlog
	Team velocity
	Constraints
	Team capabilities
	Initial sprint goal

Approaches to sprint planning,
1. Two-part sprint planning
	Separate planning into two parts.
	- The "What" part
	- The "How" part

#### Capacity
capacity refers to the total amount of work a team can complete in a given time frame.
Ex: 
	Sprint time : 10 days
	1 day for sprint-planning, sprint-review and sprint retrospective
	Up to 10% of time assisting product owner with product backlog.
	Work outside the sprint.
	Meeting times, email responding time.
After removing all these, what remains is the **Capacity** of the team to work.
Capacity can written in,
1. Story points
2. Effort hours
#### Selecting product backlog items
Several ways,
1. Align with the sprint goal.
2. Select items from the top of the backlog(if a goal is not set)
Start only what you can finish.
#### Acquiring Confidence
Use predicted velocity to see if the commitment is realistic. 
Steps,
1. Decompose the product backlog items downs into the tasks.
2. Estimate the tasks in effort-hours.
3. Subtract from the team's capacity.
4. The result is the sprint backlog.
