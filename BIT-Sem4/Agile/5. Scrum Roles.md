#### Scrum Rules
1. Teams: of 7-9 members, cross functional.
2. Time-boxes
3. Feedback and learning through frequent delivery
4. Predictability
5. Collective and adaptive planning to build the right product
6. Improve productivity and quality by
	reducing waste
	making team focus on time-boxes 

#### Scrum Roles
1. Product Owner
2. SCRUM Master
3. Development Team

### 1. Product Owner
Responsible for maximizing the value of the product by managing the **Product Backlog** and ensuring that the development team builds the right feature.
Act as a bridge between stakeholders and the development team.

The product owner must understand the need and priorities of the stakeholder to act as their representative. 

Product owner act as,
- Product manager
- Business analyst
- Feature tester

Responsibilities,
- Share the product vision/goals with the team.
- Identify the requirements 
- Prioritize requirements
- Do clarification on requirements if someone want to
- Provide feedbacks
- Negotiate with stakeholders
- Manage economics.
- Participate in planning.
- Collaborate with the development team and the stakeholders.
- Define acceptance criteria and verify them.

**Product owner team ???**
Product owner role can be handle by a team instead of a single person due to heavy workload. If so, the product owner can delegate some product owner responsibilities to other people as long as there is one person on the team who is the decision maker.

Types of Product owners(represent different roles when the traditional Product Owner (PO) role is split across multiple teams or organizational levels in large-scale Scrum implementations.),
1. Product owner - proxy
	Act as an intermediary between the real Product owner and the development team.
2. Chief Product Owner
	Found in large organizations with multiple teams working on a single product. 
	Oversees multiple product owners, ensuring alignment across teams. 
	Manage the high-level product vision.
3. Product line owner
	Responsible for an entire production line that consist of multiple related products. 
4. Feature Product Owner
	Focuses on specific feature or components of a product.

Other points,
- PO does the release planning.
- Define sprint goals.
- Creating and refining requirements.
- Requirement estimations and prioritizing. 
- Define acceptance criteria/acceptance tests for backlog items. 
- Closely work with stakeholders and the dev team.
- Every scrum team must have a product owner.

### 2. Scrum Master
Scrum master's responsibilities are designed to ensure that the scrum process is followed and that the ream works effectively within the Agile principles. 

Responsibilities,
- Coach the team on SCRUM principles. 
	for both the dev team and the product owner
	ensure the team adhere to the scrum values.
- Remove obstacles faced by the team.
- Assist the team in achieving the iteration goals.
- Act as a 'servant-leader' to the team.
- Protect the development team from outside interferences.

Scrum master doesn't have the authority that a project manager would have,
- The scrum master doesn't hire or fire.
- Can not dictate the team what task to do or how to do them.
- Nor responsible for making sure the work gets done.

### 3. Development Team
The development team is a cross-functional, self-organizing group of professionals who work together to build and deliver the product increment at the end of each sprint. 

Responsibilities, 
- Perform sprint execution.
- Inspect and adapt each day.
- Groom the product backlog. 
- Inspect and adapt the product and process.

Characteristics,
- Self-organizing
	No one tells them how to do their work. They decide how to accomplish the Sprint Goal.
- Cross functional
	The team has all the skills needed to deliver a working product increment.
- Right-sized
	Usually 3 - 9 members.
- Transparent communication
- Works at a sustainable pace
- Focused and committed

Scrum team is consist of
	Front-end developers
	Back-end developers
	QA engineers
	UI/UX designers
	DevOps engineers
	Database experts
- In a scrum development team, titles doesn't matter. Only collaboration and shared responsibility do.

### Scrum Team Structure

#### Feature Team vs Component Team
- Component team : Focus on the development of a component that can be used to create only part of an end-customer feature. 
	Ex: Database layer, UI layer, Backend services
- Feature team : A **cross-functional** team that works across multiple components to deliver **complete, end-to-end features** to users.

Scrum favors features teams over component teams. 

A member of a component team can be assigned to be a member of a feature team. Reasons,
- To promote shared code ownership within the team. 
- Collect changes that the feature team need to make within components.

### Multiple Team Coordination
Methods,
1. Scrum of Scrums
2. Release train

#### 1. Scrum of Scrum(SoS)
![[Pasted image 20250414121019.png]]
A coordination technique used when multiple scrum teams are working on a larger project and need to stay aligned.

When a single product is being built by **several Scrum Teams**, they might face:
- Overlapping work
- Dependencies
- Integration challenges

Representatives (usually one member) from each scrum team attend the scrum of scrums meetings. 
They discuss,
- Progress and roadblocks
- Dependencies between teams
- Risks or Integration issues
- What one team need from another

#### 2. Release Train
A **Release Train** is a key concept from the **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)** used to coordinate **multiple Agile teams** working on a **large product or system**.
 
An Agile Release Train(ART) is a long-lived team of agile teams(typically 5-12 teams) that,
- Work together to deliver value on a fixed schedule.
- Are aligned to a common mission, roadmap and cadence.
- Include developers, tester, UX, DevOps, product manager and more. 

Goals of a release train,
- Coordinate planning and delivery across many teams.
- Deliver integrated features/increments regularly. 
- Align business and technical teams around value.

Key elements,

| Element                     | Description                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Program Increment(PI)       | A fixed timebox (usually 8–12 weeks) in which teams deliver value. Like a “super sprint.” |
| PI Planning                 | A big planning event where all teams plan together for the next PI.                       |
| RTE(Release Train Engineer) | Like a “Chief Scrum Master” who facilitates the Release Train.                            |
| System Demo                 | A regular integrated demo showing progress from all teams.                                |
| Inspect & Adapt             | A large retrospective done at the end of a PI to improve the train's performance.         |

### Functional Managers
Scrum doesn't have manager roles. 
But managers play an important part in an agile organization. 

Functional managers are leaders responsible for a specific functions or discipline(ex: QA, UX, DevOps, Engineering) across organizations.
![[Pasted image 20250414152348.png]]

Functional managers in a SCRUM organization are responsible for,
1. Fashioning teams
	Defining boundaries.
	Form teams.
	Provide goals.
	Empower teams.
	Change team compositions.
2. Naturing teams
	Energize people.
	Develop competence.
	Provide functional leadership.
	Maintain team integrity.
3. Aligning teams
	Promote agile values.
	Remove organizational impediments.
	Align internal groups.
	Align partners. 
4. Adapting environment
	Manage economics.
	Measure and report.
5. Managing value-creation flow
	Setting strategic directions.
	Ensuring organizational resources are being used in optimal ways.
