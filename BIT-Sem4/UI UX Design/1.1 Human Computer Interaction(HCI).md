Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is ==a multidisciplinary field that studies and designs the interactions between humans and computer systems, aiming to create user interfaces that are intuitive, efficient, and enjoyable to use.==
It emerged as an area of research and practice in early 1980.
HCI has rapidly expanded and now aggregates a collection of semi-autonomous fields of research and practice in human-centered informatics.

### History of HCI
Until 1970, only IT professionals interacted with computers.
Emergence of personal computers allowed the creation of,
	personal software
	personal computer platforms
Personal computing presented the need for HCI. 
Cognitive science presented people, concepts, skills and a vision for addressing such needs through an ambitious synthesis of science and engineering.

Major concern of HCI is to design computer systems which are easy to use, easy to learn without errors.

### Human Computer Interaction
> [!NOTE] Definition 1
> Human Computer Interaction is the process of information transfer from User to Machine and Machine to User.

> [!NOTE] Definition 2
> Human-computer interaction (HCI) is a “discipline concerned with the design , evaluation and implementation interactive computing systems for human use and with the of study of major phenomena surrounding them” 
> -(ACM SIGCHI, 1992, p.6)

Goals of HCI,
- Create usable software.
- Enhance the usability of existing products.
- Identify problems and tasks that can be addressed with working software. 

HCI is an interdisciplinary field in which computer scientist, engineers, psychologists, social scientist and design professionals play important roles.

### Modern day HCI - 3 Paradigms
HCI paradigms refer to different ways of thinking about how humans interact with computers. These paradigms shows the conceptual shifts in how interaction has evolved over time. 
1. The classical(Human-factors) paradigm
2. The expressive(interaction) paradigm
3. The ubiquitous(Post-WIMP) paradigm
##### 1. The classical(Human-Factors) paradigm
**Emerged in:** 1970s-1980s 
**Influenced by:** Cognitive psychology and ergonomics
**Focuses on:** 
	Usability, 
	Efficiency, 
	minimizing errors
**Goal:** Optimize the fit between technology and man to minimize human errors.
**Question:** How can we fix problems that arise in interaction ?
**Situation:** Computers are viewed as tools to be operated efficiently.
##### 2. The expressive(Interaction) paradigm
**Emerged in:** 1990s-2000s
**Influenced by:** Advancements in interaction designs.
**Focuses on:** 
	User engagement, 
	emotions, 
	Multimodal interactions(voice, touch, gestures)
**Goal:** To bridge the gap between machine processor and human processor.
**Question:** 
	What mismatches come up in communication between computer and people.
	How can we accurately model what people do ?
	How can be improve the efficiency of computer use ?
**Examples:** 
	Touchscreen,
	VR/AR,
	AI chatbots
	gesture-based interactions
##### 3. The Ubiquitous(Post-WIMP) paradigm
**Emerged in:** 2000s - present
**Influenced by:** Advancements in interaction designs.
**Focuses on:** Pervasive computing
	Interactions become seamless, context-aware and embedded in everyday life.
**Goal:** Social sciences and user experience design.
**Question:** 
	What existing situated activities in the world should we support ?
	How do users appropriate technologies ?
	How can we support those appropriations ?
	How can we support interaction without constraining too strongly by what can computers do or understand?
	What are the values at the site of interaction and how can we support those in design ?
**Examples:** 
	IoT(Smart home),
	Voice assistants
	Brain-Computer interfaces(BCI)

### Evolution of HCI 'interfaces'
1. 50s
	Interface at the hardware level for engineers
	Ex: Switch panels
2. 60-70s
	Interface at the terminal level
	Ex: Command languages
3. 80s
	Interface at the interaction dialogue level
	Ex: GUIs, multimedia
4. 90s
	Interface at the work setting
	Ex: Network systems, groupware
5. 2000s
	Interface become pervasive
	Ex: RF tags, Bluetooth, mobile devices, consumer electronics, interactive screens, embedded technology
