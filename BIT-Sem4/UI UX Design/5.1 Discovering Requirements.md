#### Types of requirements
1. **Functional requirements**
	Capture what the product do
2. **Data requirements**
	type, volatility, size/amount, persistence, accuracy and value of the required data.
3. **Environment requirements**
	circumstances in which the interactive product will operate.
	Sub-types: 
	- Physical - requirements of the physical operational environment (ex: required electricity, noise, dust)
	- Social - public concerns (ex: Data sharing)
	- Organizational - (ex: user support, facilitate resources and user training)
	- Technical - (ex: what technologies will the product run on or need to be compatible with)
4. **User characteristics**
	key attributes of the intended user group
	(ex: User's abilities, skills, educational background, preferences, disabilities)
5. **Usability goals**
	Ex: Effectiveness, Efficiency, Safety, Easy to learn
6. **User experience goals**
	Ex: Comfortability, Aesthetic, Enjoyable, 

Ex:
Scenario : A wearable interactive product to measure glucose levels for an individual with diabetes. 
	Functions : The product will be able to take small blood samples and measure glucose readings from them.
	Data : The product will need to measure and display the glucose reading, but possibly not store it permanently and it may not need other data about the individual. 
	Environmental : The physical environment could be anywhere. The product needs to be able to cope with a wide range of conditions and situations and to be suitable for wearing.
	User characteristics : Could be any age, nationality, ability. May be a novice or an expert. 
	Usability goals : effective, efficient, safe, easy to learn and remember how to use.
	User experience goals : Device being comfortable, aesthetically pleasing, enjoyable.
