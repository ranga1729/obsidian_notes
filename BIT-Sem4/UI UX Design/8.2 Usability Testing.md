**Usability testing** is a method used to evaluate how easy and effective a product is for real users to use.

- Purpose : Identify usability issues by observing users interact with a design.
- Usually been tested in a controlled lab. Reasons,
	1. Designers can control what users do.
	2. Designers can control social influence that might impact the user's performance.

Types of Evaluations of a design
1. Controlled setting directly involving users
	Users’ activities are controlled to test hypotheses and observe certain behaviors.
	Designed to control what users do, when they do it, and for how long.
	Ex: Usability testing, Experiments
2. Natural settings involving users
	Little or no control over user activities. 
	Data takes the form conversations notes, audio, video recording or diaries.
	The goal is to be unobtrusive and not to affect what people do during the evaluation.
	Ex: Field studies
3. Any setting not directly involving users
	Evaluations take place without involving users. 
	Conducted in settings where the researcher has to imagine or model how an interface is likely to be used and identify the most obvious usability problems.
	Ex: Heuristic evaluation and walkthroughs, Analytics and A/B testing, Predictive modeling

#### Crowdsourcing
**Crowdsourcing** in UI/UX design evaluation is the process of gathering feedback or usability insights from a large group of people—usually online—to assess a design’s effectiveness.

Features,
- Uses platforms like Amazon Mechanical Turk, UserTesting, or surveys
- Collects input from a diverse group of users
- Often used to quickly test prototypes or interfaces at scale

**Heuristic evaluation** is a usability inspection method where **experts review a user interface** to identify usability problems based on **established guidelines**, called **heuristics**.

Features,
- Conducted by **UX professionals**, not end users
- Based on **Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics** (e.g., consistency, error prevention, feedback)  
- Helps detect issues early in the design process

Jakob Nielsen's 10 general principles for interaction design,
1. Visibility of System Status 
2. Match Between System and the Real World
3. User Control and Freedom
4. Consistency and Standards
5. Error Prevention 
6. Recognition Rather Than Recall 
7. Flexibility and Efficiency of Use 
8. Aesthetic and Minimalist Design 
9. Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors 
10. Help and Documentation

#### Walk-Throughs
A **walk-through** is a usability evaluation method where **designers, developers, or experts step through a system or interface** to review how a user would complete tasks.

Key points,
- Focuses on **task flows** rather than isolated screens
- Identifies **potential problems** users might face at each step
- Can be done **individually or as a group** (e.g., cognitive walkthrough)

Ex: Walk-through a login process

#### Cognitive Walk-Throughs
A **cognitive walk-through** is a usability evaluation method where **experts simulate a user’s thought process** while going through a task step by step to identify usability issues—especially for **first-time users**.

Key features,
- Focuses on **ease of learning** and **problem-solving**
- Evaluators ask at each step:
    1. Will the user know what to do?
    2. Will the user notice the correct option?
    3. Will the user understand the feedback?
#### Pluralistic Walk-Throughs
A **pluralistic walk-through** is a usability evaluation method where **designers, developers, and actual users** work together to review a user interface step by step.

Key features,
- Involves **multiple perspectives** (experts + users)
- Everyone reviews each task from the user’s point of view
- Encourages **discussion and collaboration** to identify usability problems

|Feature|**Walk-through**|**Cognitive Walk-through**|**Pluralistic Walk-through**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Purpose**|General usability review|Evaluate learnability from the user's perspective|Collaborative review from multiple perspectives|
|**Participants**|Usually experts or designers|Usability experts simulating first-time users|Designers, developers, and real users|
|**Focus**|Task flow and interface logic|User’s thought process during task completion|Real-time feedback and discussion during tasks|
|**User Involvement**|Not typically involved|Not directly involved (user simulation only)|Actively involved in the session|
|**Strength**|Quick and flexible|Good for evaluating ease of learning|Diverse insights and early user feedback|
|**Limitation**|May miss cognitive difficulties|May overlook real user behavior|Can be time-consuming and requires coordination|

#### A/B Testing
**A/B testing** is a method used in UI/UX design to **compare two versions** of a design (Version A and Version B) to see **which performs better** based on user behavior.

How it works,
- Users are **randomly shown** either version A or B
- Their actions (e.g., clicks, conversions) are **measured and compared**
- The version with **better performance metrics** is chosen
