### Cognition
Cognition refers to how users perceive, process and understand information when interacting with a digital interface. It involves mental process such as attention, memory, problem solving and decision making. These are higher level function of the brain and encompass language, imagination perception and planning.

### 3.1.1 Cognitive Aspect of a UI/UX Design
Cognitive aspect is mainly in two categories,
1. **Experiential** : Perceive, act and react to events around them. Users rely on past experiences and instincts to navigate an interface without much conscious effort.
	Ex: Instinctively tapping a familiar icon on a mobile app without thinking
2. **Reflective** : Users consciously evaluate, problem-solve, or make decisions. 
	Ex: Comparison, tooltips, explanations

Cognitions processes,
1. Attention
2. Perception
3. Memory
4. Learning
5. Reading, speaking, listening
6. Problem solving, planning, reasoning, decision making
These cognition processes are interdependent. 

#### 1. Attention
Allows people to focus on a specific stimulus in the environment. 
Difficulty of paying attention depends on,
- Clear goals
- The way information is presented

#### 2. Perception
Refers to how information is acquired from the environment via the five sense.
Information should be readily perceived. 
Ex: Grouping items together and leaving space between them.
![[Pasted image 20250402095622.png]]
#### 3. Memory
Recalling knowledge. 
	Ex: Recognize someone's face, Remember someone's name
People are better at recognizing things than recalling things. 
Some kinds of information are easier to recognize than other. 
#### 4. Learning
Connected with memory. 
Types, 
1. Incidental learning : Occurs without any intention to learn.
	Ex: Recognize faces
2. Intentional learning : Goal-directed with the goal of being able to remember it. 
	Ex: Studying for an exam
#### 5. Reading, Speaking, Listening
Depends on the person, task and context.
Support applications,
- Interactive books
- Speech recognition systems
- Speech output systems
- Natural-language processing
#### 6. Problem-solving, Planning, Reasoning, Decision making
Involving reflective cognition.
Considerations, 
- What to do ?
- Available options 
- Consequences
How people make decision with overloaded information,
1. Weighing up cost-benefits 
2. Tend to use simple heuristics
	Ex: Buying brands they easily recognize, low-priced, with attractive packaging.
Better strategy is to design technological interventions that provide just enough information in the right form for users instead of providing more information.
