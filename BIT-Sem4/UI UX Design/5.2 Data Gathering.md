The process of discovering and evaluating requirements. 
Two parts,
1. Discovering requirements
	Aim : Collect sufficient, accurate and relevant data to proceed the design process.
2. Evaluating requirements
	Capture user reactions and feedbacks with the system/prototype.

- Data gathering requires little to no programming or technical skills. 
- Main data gathering techniques,
	1. Interviews
	2. Questionaries
	3. Observations

Key issues of require attention in data gathering sessions,
1. Goal setting
2. Identifying participants
	Identifying people from whom is to be data gathered, AKA sampling. 
3. The relationship between the data collector and the data provider
	Can be achieved by asking participants to sign an informed consent.
4. Triangulation
	**Triangulation of data** : Draw data from different sources at different times, in different places, or from different people possibly by using a different sampling technique.
	**Investigator triangulation** : Employee Different researchers, observers, interviewers collect and interpret the data.
	**Triangulation of theories** : Use of different theoretical frameworks to view the data.
	**Methodological triangulation** : Use different data gathering techniques.
5. Pilot studies
	A small trial run of the main study. 
	Aim : Make sure the proposed method is viable.
##### Identifying participants
We have to define the type of people from whom data is to be gathered.
Those people who fit into this profile is called the **population** or **study population**.
Choosing the people who fit into this profile is called **sampling**.

Two main sampling methods,
1. Probability sampling
2. Non-probability sampling
#### 1. Probability Sampling
1. Random sampling
Choose people by using a random number generator or by choosing every nth person in a list. 
![[Pasted image 20250509160204.png]]
2. Systematic sampling
Similar to simple random sampling.
Every member is listed with a number and individuals are chosen at regular intervals. 
![[Pasted image 20250509161521.png]]
3. Stratified Sampling
Divide the population into groups and then applying random sampling. 
![[Pasted image 20250509162053.png]]
4. Cluster sampling
Divide population into subgroups. 
Each subgroup should have similar characteristics to the whole sample. 
Randomly select entire subgroups. 
![[Pasted image 20250509162239.png]]
#### 2. Non-probability Sampling
1. Convenience sampling
A situation where the sample include those who were available rather than those specifically selected. 
![[Pasted image 20250509162514.png]]
2. Snowball sampling
A current participant finds another participants and that participant finds another, and so on.
![[Pasted image 20250509162805.png]]
3. Purposive sampling
AKA **Judgement sampling**.
Involves the researcher using their expertise to select a sample.

### Recording Data
Data recording method will be depends on,
1. The goal of the study
2. How the data will be used
3. The context
4. The time
5. Resource available
6. Sensitivity of the situation.

Common data recording approaches:
### Interviews
Types of interviews,
1. Open-minded/unstructured : Similar to conversation around a particular topic.
2. Structured : Focus on a predetermined questions similar to those in a questionnaire. Typically closed questions.
3. Semi-structured : Combined features of structured and unstructured. Has a basic script and start with preplanned questions. 
4. Group interviews

The most appropriate type to interview depends on,
1. Purpose of the interview
2. Question to be asked

### Focus Groups
- Interview people in a group.
- Participants are selected to provide a representative sample of the target population. 
- Aim : Identify conflicts in expectations or terminology from different stakeholders. 
- Allow diverse or sensitive issues to be raised.
### Questionnaires
- Good technique for collecting demographic data and user's opinions. 
- Can have open or closed ended questions. 
- Effort and skills are needed to ensure that questions are clearly worded and the data collected can be analyzed efficiently.
### Observations
- Users are observed directly by the investigator as they perform their activities or indirectly through records of the activities.
- Individuals are observed as they go about their day-to-day tasks in the natural setting or individuals are observed performing specified tasks within a controlled environment.
- Help designers understand the users' context, tasks and goals.
##### Degree of participation
Degree of participation means the level of involvement and influence a person or group has in a decision-making process or activity.
An observer who adopts an approach as an outsider is called a **Passive observer**.
An observer who adopts an approach as an insider is called a **Participant observer**.
##### Ethnography
- Ethnography is the scientific description of peoples and cultures with their customs, habits, and mutual differences.
- Used in the social sciences to uncover the organization of societies and their activities.
- A large part of most ethnographic studies is done by direct observations.

**The think aloud technique**
Requires people to say out loud everything that they thinking and trying to do.

- When direct observations are not possible, indirect observations can be done. 
	Ex: Diaries, Interaction logs

### Contextual Inquiry
Contextual inquiry is the core field research process for contextual design. 
Involves one-on-one field interviews, AKA **Contextual Interviews**.
These interviews focus on matters of daily life that are relevant for the project scope. 
	User acts as a **Master** and they **do** the work while talking. 
	The interviewer act as an **apprentice** and learns what is been done instead of asking questions. 

#### Brainstorming
Generate, refine and develop ideas. 
Used for alternative designs or for suggesting new and better ideas to support users. 

For a successful brainstorming session,
1. Include participants from a wide range of disciplines with a broad range of experience.
2. Don't ban silly ideas
3. Keep records
4. Sharpen the focus
5. Use warm-up exercises and make the session fun
### Augmenting Requirements
User stories fails to communicate the products purpose and vision sufficiently. 
Solutions,
1. Requirements should be augmented with prototypes, working systems, screenshots, conversations, acceptance criteria and diagrams. 
2. Create UXD requirements,
	Personas, Scenarios
#### Personas
User personas are fictional characters that represent key segments of your target audience. They are created based on user research and include details like demographics, goals, behaviors, and pain points. Personas help design teams keep real users in mind when making decisions.

Guideline for creating effective personas,
1. Collect extensive data on target users. 
2. Determine the qualities of and difference between users.
3. Develop a hypothesis from the research, determining the qualities of and differences between users.
4. Ensure stakeholders agree on the hypothesis about the users. 
5. Determine a number of personas. 
6. Describe each persona including a picture, users values, interests, needs, attitudes, desires, limitations, goals, and behavior patterns.
7. Describe several situations/scenarios prompting the persona to use your product.
8. Involve everyone to review personas.
9. Make continuous adjustments.
