#### Prototyping
Prototyping allows 
	designers to communicate their ideas
	users to try them out
	stakeholders to interact and explore its suitability.

A prototype can be anything from a paper based storyboard to a complex piece of software.
![[Pasted image 20250510161139.png]]

**Why prototypes,**
- Used to test the technical feasibility of an idea. 
- Clarify vague requirements.
- user testing and evaluation 
- compatibility with the rest of the system

- A storyboard represents a sequence of actions or events that the user and the product go through to achieve a goal. 
- A scenario is one story about how a product may be used to achieve that goal.

**Design Maps/Journey Map/Experience Map**
- A set of card-based prototypes that cover a scenario from beginning to end.
- May be used in conjunction with personas to explore the user’s end-to-end experience.
- Illustrate a user’s path or journey through the product or service and are usually created for a particular persona and based on a particular scenario.
Two main types,
1. The wheel : used when an interaction phase is more important than an interaction point

2. The timeline : used when a service is being provided that has a recognizable beginning and end point

#### Wizard of OZ
![[Pasted image 20250510202313.png]]
The wizard of Oz(WoZ) technique in prototyping is a method where a user interacts with a system that they believe to be autonomous, but it is actually being operated or partially controlled by a human behind the scenes.(the "Wizard")

Usage,
1. To simulate and test user interactions with a system before it is fully built. 
2. Helps gather feedback on design, usability, and user expectations without requiring a fully functioning system. 

Benefits,
1. Save time and costs in real development.
2. Lets designers observe natura user behavior.
3. Helps refine features before actual implementation.

#### Different development philosophies
1. Evolutionary prototyping
A prototype evolves into the final product and is build with these engineering principles in mind. 
Each stage will be subjected to rigorous testing.

2. Throwaway prototyping
Uses the prototypes as steppingstones toward the final design.
The prototypes are thrown away, and the final product is built from scratch.
