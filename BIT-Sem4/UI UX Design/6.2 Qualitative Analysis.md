Three basic approaches to qualitative analysis,
1. Inductive Analysis
2. Deductive Analysis
3. Analyzing Critical Incidents

![[Pasted image 20250510115028.png]]
**1. Inductive Analysis**
- "Themes" are repeating ideas, topics or patterns appear in qualitative data. 
- Inductive analysis starts with raw data (like interviews or open-ended survey responses) and let themes naturally emerge from the data.
- Purpose : To discover patterns, insights, or concepts that are not predetermined.
- Useful when you don’t have a hypothesis and want to explore open-ended data.
- A bottom to top approach.
Ex: After reading 20 interview transcripts, you notice many people mention "lack of time" - This becomes a theme. 

**2. Deductive Analysis**
- Begin with a set of predefined categories based on theory or prior research, and sort data accordingly. 
- Purpose : To test existing ideas or theories using qualitative data. 
- A top-down approach.
Ex: Responses from a research done on 'stress' can be categorized into know causes like "workload", "family issues" and "financial problems"

**3. Analyzing Critical Incidents**
- Focus on key events or moments that have a strong impact.
- Purpose : To gain deep insight into events that are particularly revealing or meaningful.
Ex: Analyzing a incident, a nurse describes a moment when a patient collapsed unexpectedly, to improve emergency protocols.

##### Identifying Themes(Thematic analysis)
A common technique for identifying themes and looking for an overall narrative is to create an affinity diagram. 

![[Pasted image 20250510120642.png]]
An affinity diagram is a visual tool used to organize a large number of ideas or data points into meaningful groups or categories based on their inherent relationships.

Steps,
1. Put observations/ideas/data into notes.
2. Take one note and make it the first note of the first group.
3. Take the next note and compare it with the first note.
	If there's a similarity, place it in the first group or place it in it's own group.
	Continue this step. 
4. Once you have several groups, cluster them based on similarities. 
5. Rank the clusters based on their importance. 
6. Create connection between clusters using lines.
7. Describe what synthesized(insights, user needs, pain points or gaps you haven's addressed yet).
