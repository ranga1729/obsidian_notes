Project integration management is all about ensuring that the various elements of a project are properly coordinated. 
It’s how you make sure that all parts of the project including planning, execution, monitoring, and closing, work together smoothly to meet the project's goals.
PMI is one of the ten knowledge areas in project management as defined by the **PMBOK Guide(Project Management Body of Knowledge)**.

Key processes,
1. Develop project charter.
	Work with stakeholders to create the document(the project charter) that formally authorizes a project. It gives the project manager authority and defines high-level goals and stakeholders.
2. Develop preliminary project scope statement.
	Work with stakeholders, especially users of the project's products, services or result to develop the high-level scope requirements and create a preliminary project scope statement. 
3. Develop project management plan.
	Combines all subsidiary plans (scope, time, cost, quality) into a consistent, cohesive plan.
4. Direct and manage project execution.
	Performing the tasks in the project management plan.
	Delivering outputs, managing teams and using resources.
5. Monitor and control project work.
	Tracking performance.
	Identifying any issues or risks and making necessary changes.
6. Integrated change control.
	Managing changes to the project scope, schedule or cost in a controlled way. Ensures every change is evaluated before approval.
7. Close project.
	Finalize all project activities to formally close the project.
	Get stakeholders approval and archive documents.

### Strategic Planning and Project Selection
Strategic planning involves 
	determining long-term objectives, 
	predicting future trends, 
	projecting the future needs.

Organization perform a **SWOT** analysis.
	S : Strength
	W : Weaknesses
	O : Opportunity
	T : Threats

As a part of the strategic planning, organizations should,
	Identify potential projects.
	Use realistic methods to select which projects to work on.
	Formalize project initiation by issuing a project charter.

### Methods for Selecting Projects
Methods,
1. Focusing on broad organizational needs.
2. Categorizing IT projects.
3. Performing net present value or other financial analyses. 
4. Using a weighted scoring model.
5. Implementing a balanced scorecard.

##### 1. Focusing on broad organizational needs
Criteria,
1. Need for the project.
2. Funds availability.
3. Strong will to make the project. 
##### 2. Categorizing IT projects.
Categorization can be based on several basis.
Ex: Time, Priority, Response(Outcome)

Ex: Categorization assesses whether the project provides a response to,
	A problem.
	An opportunity.
	A directive. 
##### 3. Performing Financial Analyses
Primary methods for determining the financial value of projects,
1. Net present value(NPV) analysis
	Calculating the expected net monetary gain or loss from a project by discounting all expected future cash inflows and outflows to the present point in time. The higher the NPV, the better. 
	![[Pasted image 20250415131856.png]]
	![[Pasted image 20250415131916.png]]
	![[Pasted image 20250415132240.png]]
	Determine estimated costs and benefits for the life of the project and the products it produces. 
	Determine the discount rate. 
	Calculate the NPV.
2. Return on investment(ROI)
	Calculated by subtracting the project costs from the benefits and then dividing by the costs. 
	![[Pasted image 20250415132526.png]]
	Higher the ROI, the better. 
	Many organizations have a minimum required rate of ROI.
3. Payback analysis
	The payback period is the amount of time it will take to recoup, in the form of net cash inflows, the total dollars invested in a project. Payback occurs when the cumulative discounted benefits and costs are greater than zero.
