Procurement means acquiring goods and/or services from an outside source.
Why Organizations Choose to Outsource,
1. To reduce both fixed and recurrent costs
2. To allow the client organization to focus on its core business
3. To access skills and technologies
4. To provide flexibility
5. To increase accountability

A contract is a legal mutually binding agreement that obligates the seller to provide the specified products/services and obligates the buyer to pay for them.
Benefits,
- Clarify responsibilities
- Sharpen focus on key deliverables
- Increase accountability (legally binding)

### 1. Planning Purchases and Acquisitions
Determining what to procure, when, and how.
Identifying which project needs can best be met by using products/services from outside the organization.
Tools and Techniques,
- **Make-or-buy analysis**: Financial technique to determine whether to make in-house or buy externally
- **Expert judgment**: Internal and external experts provide valuable inputs
> [!NOTE] Example
> Assume you can lease an item you need for a project for $800/day. 
> To purchase the item, the cost is $12,000 plus a daily operational cost of $400/day.
> 
> How long it take for the purchase cost to be the same as the lease cost ?
> $12,000 + $400d = $800d
> $12,000 = $400d
> d = 30
> Therefore, If you need the item for more than 30 days, it is more economical to purchase it. 
### 2. Planning Contracting
Describing requirements for products/services desired.
Identifying potential sources or sellers (contractors, suppliers, providers)
Key documents for the contract can be depend on the project needs but they must define how the acquisition will be made. 
##### Contract Statement of Work (SOW)
SOW(Statement of Work) is the description of work required for procurement and if the SOW is used as a part of a contract it's called the 'Contract Statement of Work'.
SOW is type of a cope statement.
It gives bidders better understanding of buyer's expectations

SOW template,
- Scope of work : Describe the work to be done.
- Location of work : Where the work must be performed.
- Period of performance : Specify the start and end dates, working hours, bill periods and other schedule information.
- Deliverables schedule : List of deliverables and their dues.
- Applicable standards
- Acceptance criteria
- Special requirements : (ex: hardware or software certifications, minimum degree or experience level of personnel)

    - Advertising to anyone interested

4. Awarding the contract

### 5. Administering the Contract
Managing the relationship with selected seller and ensuring seller's performance meets contractual requirements.
- **Change Control Suggestions**:
    - Review and approve changes through the same people who in the same way that the original part of the plan was approved
    - Include impact analysis for any change
    - Document changes in writing
    - Stay involved to ensure system meets business needs
    - Have backup plans
    - Use tools like contract change control systems, performance reviews, inspections and audits
### 6. Closing the Contract
Completing and settling contracts and resolving any open items.
Tasks,
- Determine if work was completed correctly and satisfactorily
- Update records to reflect final results
- Archive information for future use
Tools
- Procurement audits : To identify lessons learned in the procurement process
- Records management system : For document organization

### Types of Contracts
1. Fixed price or lump sum
2. cost reimbursable
3. Time and material
4. Unit price
##### 1. Fixed Price (Lump Sum) Contracts
Involve fixed total price for well-defined product/service. Lowest risk for buyer, highest for seller.
##### 2. Cost Reimbursable Contracts
Involve payment to seller for direct and indirect costs
Types:
1. Cost Plus Incentive Fee (CPIF): Costs + predetermined fee + incentive bonus
2. Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF): Costs + fixed fee (usually percentage of estimated costs)
3. Cost Plus Percentage of Costs (CPPC): Costs + predetermined percentage of total costs

##### 3. Time and Material Contracts
Hybrid of fixed price and cost reimbursable contracts. Often used by consultants
##### 4. Unit Price Contracts
Require buyer to pay predetermined amount per unit of service
## Important Contract Elements
- **Termination clause**: Allows buyer or supplier to end the contract
- Specific clauses for issues unique to the project
- Requirements for formal acceptance and closure
