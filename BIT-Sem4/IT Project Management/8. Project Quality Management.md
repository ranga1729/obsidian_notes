
> [!NOTE] Definition of Quality 
> ISO(International Organization for Standardization) defines quality as **"The degree to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfils requirements"**.
> (ISO9000:2000)
> 
> Other definitions,
> 1. Conformance to requirements(in which degree product meets with it's specifications)
> 2. Fitness fit use

Project quality management ensures that the project will satisfy the needs for which it was undertaken.

Project quality management process,
1. Quality planning : Identifying relevant quality standards and how to satisfy them
2. Quality assurance : Periodically evaluating project performance
3. Quality control : Monitoring specific project results for compliance with quality standards

*****
#### 1. Quality Planning
Focuses on preventing defects through proper materials, training, and process planning. 
Uses techniques like **design of experiments**(DoE) to identify influential variables. 

> [!NOTE] Design of Experiment(DoE)
> Statistical approach used to identify and analyze the factors that influence a process or product's quality. Involves documenting important factors that directly contribute to meeting customer requirements.
> 

Quality aspect of an IT project,
- Functionality: How well a system performs intended functions
- Features: Special characteristics appealing to users
- System outputs: Screens and reports generated
- Performance: How well the product meets intended use
- Reliability: Performance under normal conditions
- Maintainability: Ease of performing maintenance

- Project mangers are ultimately responsible for quality management on the project. 
	Can follow standards from,
	- ISO
	- IEEE

#### 3. Controlling Quality
Main outputs of quality control,
1. Acceptance decisions
2. Rework
3. Process adjustments

Tools and techniques,
1. **Preto analysis** : Identifies vital few contributors
	AKA 80-20 rules.
	Pareto diagrams : histograms or column charts. 
	Representing a frequency distribution, that help identify and prioritize problem areas.
	ex: 
	![[Pasted image 20250516191126.png]]
2. **Statistical sampling** : Choosing sample of a population for inspection
	$$\text{Sample size} = 0.25 \times (\frac{\text{Certainity factor}}{\text{Acceptable error}})^2$$
3. **Six sigma** : data-driven methodology used in quality control to reduce defects and improve processes by identifying and eliminating variation.
4. **Testing** : Should be done during almost every phase of the development.
	Unit testing : Test each individual component
	Integration testing : between unit and system testing to test functionally grouped components
	System testing : tests the entire system as one entity
	User acceptance testing : test with end users
5. **Quality control charts**
	Ex: Gantt charts for testing

### Modern Quality Management 
Key points,
- Requires customer satisfaction
- Prefers prevention over inspection
- Recognizes management responsibility

Notable quality experts,
- Deming: 14 Points for Management
- Juran: Quality Control Handbook
- Crosby: "Quality is Free," zero defects concept
- Ishikawa: Quality circles, fishbone diagrams
- Taguchi: Engineering experimentation optimization
- Feigenbaum: Total quality control

ISO standards,
1. ISO 9000
	A three-part continuous cycle of planning, controlling and documenting quality in an organization.
	Provides minimum requirements needed for an organization to meet its quality certification standards. • Helps organizations around the world reduce costs and improve customer satisfaction.
2. ISO 15504
	AKA SPICE(Software Process Improvement and Capability Determination).
	A framework for the assessment of software processes.

Suggestions for improving quality of IT projects,
1. Establish leadership that promotes quality.
2. Understand the cost of quality.
3. Focus on organizational influence and workplace factors that affect quality.
4. Follow maturity models. 
##### Cost of Quality
$$
\text{Cost of quality} = \text{Cost of conformance + Cost of nonconformance}
$$
- Conformance : Delivering products that meet requirements and fitness for use
- Cost of Non-conformance : Taking responsibility for failures or not meeting quality expectations.
Cost categories, 
 1. **Prevention cost**: Cost of Planning for error-free execution
 2. **Appraisal cost**: Cost of Evaluating processes and outputs
 3. **Internal failure cost**: Cost of Correcting defects before delivery
 4. **External failure cost**: Cost of Errors detected after delivery
 5. **Measurement/test equipment costs**: Cost of Capital costs for equipment
