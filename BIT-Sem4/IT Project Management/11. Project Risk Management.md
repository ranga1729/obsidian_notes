### Understanding Risk
- **Risk** : The possibility of loss or injury.
- **Negative Risk**: Risks that might negatively affect project success
- **Positive Risk(Opportunity)**: Risks that result in good outcomes

The general definition of project risk is an uncertainty that can have a negative or positive effect on meeting project objectives.

Goal of project risk management : 
	Minimize potential negative risks
	Maximize potential positive risks

**Risk Utility/Risk Tolerance**
Risk utility/risk tolerance refers to the amount of satisfaction/pleasure/gain a project manager or stakeholder receives from a potential payoff or benefit, considering the associated risks. 
- **Risk-averse**: Tend to prioritize avoiding negative outcomes and will choose options with lower risks and predictable outcomes over those with higher potential rewards but also higher potential losses.
- **Risk-seeking**: Higher tolerance for risk with increased satisfaction for higher payoffs. More comfortable with uncertain outcomes and may choose options with higher potential rewards, even if it means accepting higher potential risks.
- **Risk-neutral**: Aims to find a balance between risk and reward, ensuring that potential gains outweigh potential losses.

### Project Risk Management Processes
Key components,
1. Risk management planning
2. Risk identification
3. Qualitative risk analysis
4. Quantitative risk analysis
5. Risk response planning
6. Risk monitoring and control
### 1. Risk Management Planning
Deciding how to approach and plan the risk management activities for the project.
Main output : Risk Management Plan 
	Documents procedures for managing risk throughout a project.

Topics addressed in a risk management plan:
- Methodology
- Roles and responsibilities
- Budget and schedule
- Risk categories
- Risk probability and impact
- Risk documentation
##### Contingency plan, Fallback plans and Contingency reserves
- **Contingency plans**: Predefined actions if identified risks occur
- **Fallback plans**: Developed for high-impact risks when risk reduction fails
- **Contingency reserves**: Provisions/funds held to reduce cost of risks
### 2. Risk Identification
Process of understanding what events might hurt or enhance a project
Common tools and techniques to identify risks:
- Brainstorming
- Delphi Technique
- Interviewing
- SWOT analysis

Category of risks,
1. Marker risk
2. Financial risk
3. People risk
4. Structure/process risk

Common Sources of IT Project Risks,
- User involvement issues
- Lack of executive management support
- Unclear requirements
- Poor planning
- Unrealistic expectations

RBS is a hierarchy of potential risk categories for a project. Similar to WBS(Work Breakdown Structure). Helps identify and categorize risks systematically.

### 3. Qualitative Risk Analysis
Assesses the likelihood and impact of identified risks to determine priority.
Tools and techniques include:
1. **Probability/Impact Matrix**: 
	A chart showing the relative probability of a risk occurring on one side of a matrix and the relative impact of the risk occurring on the other.
	Can calculate the "Risk Factor" that represent the overall risk of specific risk events based on their probability of occurring and the consequences.

2. **Top Ten Risk Item Tracking**: Helps identify and maintain awareness of risks throughout the project.
	Establish periodic review of the top ten project risk items. Type of a qualitative risk analysis method.

3. **Expert Judgment**: Utilizing intuition and experience of experts to categorize risks
### 4. Quantitative Risk Analysis
- Often follows qualitative analysis but can be done simultaneously
- Often used by large, complex projects with leading-edge technologies
- Main techniques:
    - **Decision Tree Analysis**: Diagramming technique to select the best course of action
    - **Expected Monetary Value (EMV)**: Product of risk probability and monetary value. Calculated using a decision tree.
	    ![[Pasted image 20250518104101.png]]
    - **Simulation**
    - **Sensitivity Analysis**
### 5. Risk Response Planning
- Taking steps to enhance opportunities and reduce threats to project objectives
### 6. Risk Monitoring and Control
- Monitoring identified and residual risks
- Identifying new risks
- Carrying out risk response plans
- Evaluating effectiveness of risk strategies

## Risk Management Tools
- Risk registers (can be created in Word, Excel, or databases)
- Specialized risk management software (Monte Carlo simulation tools)
- PMI Risk Specific Interest Group resources (risksig.com)
## Benefits of Good Project Risk Management
- Well-run projects appear almost effortless
- Prevents the need for crisis management
- Makes project management look easy
## Risks by Knowledge Area
- **Integration**: Inadequate planning, poor resource allocation
- **Scope**: Poor definition, incomplete quality requirements
- **Time**: Estimation errors, poor float management
- **Cost**: Estimating errors, inadequate productivity/cost control
- **Quality**: Poor attitudes, substandard design/materials
- **Human Resources**: Poor conflict management, lack of leadership
- **Communications**: Carelessness in planning/communicating
- **Risk**: Ignoring risks, unclear risk assignment
- **Procurement**: Unenforceable conditions, adversarial relations
