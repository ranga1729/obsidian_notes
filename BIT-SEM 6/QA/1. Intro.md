A software is any set of machine-readable instruction to the processor to perform a specific operation. 
Software quality is,
- The degree to which a system, components or process meets specified requirements. 
- The degree to which a system, components or process meets customer or user needs or expectations. 

Quality assurance is a part of a long concept called quality management. Testing is more of a quality control activity. QA associated with ensuring that a company's standard ways of performing various tasks are carried out correctly. 


> [!NOTE] Definition of Software Quality Assurance by IEEE
> Software quality assurance is,
> 1. A planned and systematic pattern of all actions necessary to provide adequate confidence that an item or product conforms to established technical requirements.
> 2. A set of activities designed to evaluate the process by which the products are developed or manufactured

SQA must not limited to the development process. It should be extended to cover the long years of service and support.
SQA should not be limited to technical aspects of functional requirements, but include activities that deal with scheduling and the budgets as well.
#### Software Quality Assurance vs Software Quality Control
**Quality control** 
	A set of activities designed to evaluate the quality of a developed product. 
	Main objective = Withhold of any product that does not qualify.
**Quality Assurance** 
	A set of activities which detect and prevent the cause of errors and correct errors early in the development process. 
	Main objective = Reduce the cost of guaranteeing quality in a product. 

Quality control activities are only a part of the total range of quality assurance activities. 

#### Objectives of SQA in development
1. Assuring the software will satisfy the functional and technical requirements.
2. Assuring the software will satisfy scheduling and budgetary requirements.
3. Taking actions to improve the software development process and QA activities. 
#### Objectives of SQA in maintenance
1. Assuring the maintenance activities are satisfying the functional and technical requirements of the system. 
2. Assuring the maintenance activities are satisfying the scheduling and budgetary requirements. 
3. Taking actions to improve the software maintenance process and QA activities. 
#### Objectives of testing
1. To verify all the specified requirements have been fulfilled.
2. To find defects. (primary target)
3. Provide information to stakeholders to make decision on quality. 
4. To reduce the level of risk of inadequate software quality.
5. To comply with legal or regulatory requirements and standards. 
#### Testing vs Debugging
**Debugging**
	Find a defect, analyze it and remove it. 
**Testing**
	Detect system defects and the failures which are caused by the defects.

After debugging, further testing has to be applied to ensure the system is functioning without any failures.

#### Errors, Faults and Failures
**Error**(The mistake)
	A mistake made by a person during the software development process.
	Ex: Accidentally type `i>10` instead of `i>=10`, typos, logical errors
**Faults**(The flaw/bug)
	Commonly calls a bug/defect. It sits silently in the codebase until that specific code is executed.
	Ex: Since `if(i>10)` in the code, a part of the program functions improperly. 
**Failures**(The event)
	An event where the software deviates from its expected behavior or fails to perform its required function. A failure occurs only a runtime when an end-user runs the software. 
	Ex: The software skipped running the 10th time which it suppose to, broken UI or missing item, observable malfunctions.

Only a portion of errors becomes faults. 
Only a portion of faults become failures.

#### Client-developer communication failures
Causes,
1. Misunderstanding of client's instructions
2. Misunderstanding of client's requirement changes
3. Misunderstanding of client's responses to design changes
4. Lack of attention to client messages regarding requirement changes. 
#### Deviations from original Software requirements,
Causes,
1. Reusing modules from other projects without sufficient analysis.
2. Omit some parts due to time or budget pressures. 
3. Developers do improvements without client's approval.
#### Logical Design errors,
Causes,
1. Erroneous algorithms. 
2. Software process definitions with sequencing errors. 
3. Erroneous definitions of the software process.
4. Omission of required system states. 
#### Coding errors
Causes,
1. Misunderstood software designs
2. Linguistic errors in programming languages.
3. Errors in design/development tools. 
4. Errors in data. 
#### Faulty testing process
Causes,
1. Incomplete test plans
2. Failures in documenting and reporting errors and faults
3. Failures in correcting detected errors
4. Incomplete corrections of detected errors.

### Seven Testing Principles
1. Testing shows the presence of defects, not their absence. 
	Testing can not prove that there are no defects. It can only show that defects are present. Testing reduces the probability of undiscovered defects in software.
2. Exhaustive testing is impossible
	Testing all combinations of inputs and preconditions is not feasible. Instead of testing exhaustively, risk analysis, test techniques, and test priorities should be considered further.
3. Early testing save time and money
	Testing early in the software development life cycle helps reduce or eliminate costly changes.
4. Defects cluster together
	Usually, a small number of modules contain the majority of the defects discovered. These defect clusters are responsible for most of the operational failures
5. Beware of the pesticide paradox
	If the same tests are repeated again and again, eventually these tests lose the ability to find new errors. Therefore, in order to find new errors, tests and test data need to keep changing and new tests should be written.
6. Testing is context dependent
	Testing should be done differently in different contexts by considering the properties of the underlying system.
7. Absence of errors is a fallacy
	Since exhaustive testing is impossible(principle 2), and testing shows the presence of defects, not their absence(principle 1), running all the tests and finding all the defects is a fallacy. Also, just finding and fixing a large number of defects does not ensure a successful system.

### SQA Models and Standards
1. **ISO 9000-3**
	Emphasizes eight principles related to quality management, 
	- Customer focus
	- Leadership
	- Involvement of people
	- Process approach
	- System approach to management 
	- Continual improvement 
	- Factual approach to decision making
	- Mutually supportive supplier relationship.
Organizations required to complete following in order to get ISO 9000-3
- Develop the organization's SQA system
	By developing quality manuals and a comprehensive set of SQA procedures. 
	By staff training, good documentation and quality records. 
- Implement the organizations SQ system. 
	By setting up staff instruction programs and support services for efficient problem-solving in implementing SQA tools.
- Undergo certification audits
	By reviewing the quality manuals and SQA procedures developed by the organization.
	By verifying the requirements specified by the organizations in their quality manuals and SQA procedures. 44

#### Capability Maturity Models(CMM)
An initiative suggested by Carnegie Mellon University in 1986 which mainly focuses on developing and refining an organization’s software development process.
Focuses on adding agile principles to CMM to improve development processes, software configuration management, and software quality management.
#### Bootstrap Model
Measures the maturity of an organization and its projects on the basis of 31 quality attributes categorized into three classes; process, organization, and technology.
