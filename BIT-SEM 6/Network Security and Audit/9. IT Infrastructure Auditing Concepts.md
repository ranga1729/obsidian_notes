### The need for Information Systems security Compliance
- Compliance mapping to external regulations and industry standards. 
- Failure to comply with external regulations and industry standards can cause penalties. 

#### IT Security Assessment
IT security assessment is a key activity that involves the "management of risk".

A risk-based approach to managing information security,
- Identifying and categorizing the information and the information systems
- Selecting and implementing appropriate security controls
- Assessing the controls for effectiveness
- Authorizing the systems by accepting the risk based upon the selected security controls
- Monitoring the security controls on a continual basis

Security controls include the physical, procedural, and technical mechanisms to safeguard systems.

A security assessment should produce information required to do the following,
- Identify weaknesses within the controls implemented on information systems 
- Confirm that previously identified weaknesses have been remediated or mitigated 
- Prioritize further decisions to mitigate risks. 
- Provide assurance, a level of confidence that effective controls are in place and that associated risks are accepted and authorized.
- Provide support and planning for future budgetary requirements.

The person who assessing the security can be external or internal.

NIST provides a framework for security assessment plans, 
- Assessment objectives 
	Each objective has a set of assessment methods(Ex: examination, interview, and test)
	Each objective has a set of assessment objects(Ex: specification, mechanism, activity, and individual)

#### IT Security Audit
IT security audit is an independent assessment of an organization’s internal policies, controls, and activities. 

Scope of the IT audit can be, 
1. Organizational - examines the management control over IT and related programs, policies, and processes.
2. Compliance - ensuring that specific guidelines, laws, or requirements have been met.
3. Applications - involves the applications that are strategic
4. Technical - This examines the IT infrastructure and data communications.

An effective IT security audit program should accomplish three goals, 
1. Provide an objective and independent review of an organization’s policies, information systems, and controls.
2. Ensure effective IT controls are in place.
3. Provide recommendations for both corrective actions and improvement. 

External Auditors 
	Independent of the organization.
	Often engage with accounting/consulting firms. 
	Limited to providing information. 
Internal Auditors
	Employed by the organization. 
	Not independent of the organization.
	Directly report to the board of directors. 
	Provide recommendations for improvements. 

Internal Compliance
	Refers to an organization’s ability to follow its own rules, which are typically based on defined policies
External compliance
	refers to the need for an organization to follow rules and guidelines set by external organizations

The general steps to meeting compliance,
1. Interpret the regulati0n and how it applies to the organization.
2. Identify the gap. 
3. Create a plan to close the gap
4. Execute the plan.

Auditing vs Assessing 
1. Auditors should not audit systems they designed.
2. Audits are independent evaluations. 
3. Audits follow a rigorous approach and are conducted according to accepted principles.
4. Auditors needs to be qualified. 
5. When an organization passes an audit, the organization typically receives a certification or confirmation. 
6. An audit is concerned about past results and performance.
	Assessment considers previous and current results as well as expected performance.

#### COBIT(Control Objective for Information and Related Technology)
Its a globally recognized IT governance and management framework developed by ISACA. It provides a structured set of best practices and controls that help organizations align their technology initiatives with overarching business goals, manage cybersecurity risks, and ensure regulatory compliance.

COBIT helps to align IT with the business or enterprise requirements by doing the following, 
1. Mapping controls to key business requirements.
2. Classifying IT activities into a process model
3. Identifying the key IT resources to be controlled
4. Defining the framework for control objectives

COBIT 5 principles, 
1. Meeting stakeholder needs
	Categories,
	- Benefits realizations
	- Risk optimization
	- Resource optimization
2. Covering the enterprise End-to-end
	Elements,
	- Governance enablers 
	- Governance scope 
	- Roles, activities, and relationships
3. Applying a single integrated framework
4. Enabling holistic approach
	Seven category of enablers,
	- Principles, policies, and frameworks
	- Processes
	- Organizational structures
	- Culture, ethics, and behavior
	- Information
	- Service, infrastructure, and applications
	- People, skills, and competencies
	Each enabler has, 
	- Stakeholders
	- Goals
	- Lifecycle
	- Good practice
5. Separating governance from management
	- Governance ensures that stakeholder needs are evaluated and ready to be achieved.
	- Management plans, builds, runs and monitors activities in alignment with the direction set by the governance body.
#### SOC(Service Organization Control)
**SOC** (originally **Service Organization Control**, and now officially called **System and Organization Controls**) is a suite of voluntary compliance standards developed by the **American Institute of CPAs (AICPA)**. It verifies that a service provider securely manages your data to protect your organization's interests and privacy.

Primary stakeholders for SOC reports, 
- User entities
- Service organizations
- Auditors
Primary types of SOC reports,
- SOC 1(Internal Control Over Financial Reporting - ICFR) : Focuses solely on the controls a service organization has in place that may affect their clients' financial statements.
- SOC 2(Trust Services Criteria) : Focuses on a service provider’s non-financial controls relating to **Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy**. This is the industry standard for SaaS and IT service providers.
- SOC 3 : A high-level, general-use summary of the SOC 2 report. It is typically published on a company’s website as a "seal of trust" for the general public, rather than a detailed technical report.

#### Consensus Audit Guideline(CAG)
Originally established in 2008 by the SANS Institute and a consortium of federal and private security experts, the initiative was called the Consensus Audit Guidelines. The goal was to build a security standard based directly on _actual real-world attacks_ rather than theoretical compliance. Today, these guidelines are maintained by the Center for Internet Security (CIS) and are referred to as the CIS Critical Security Controls.

This is a cyber security framework now known as the "Center for Internet Security(CIS)".
