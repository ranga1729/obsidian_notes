## Forensic Science
Forensic Science is a scientific discipline that applies scientific methods and principles to the investigation of crimes and legal issues.

It involves,
- Collection
- Analysis 
- Interpretation 
of physical evidences such as blood, fingerprints, DNA, fibers to help determine the facts of a case. 

Forensic Science is has specialized areas, 
- Forensic Biology
- Forensic Chemistry 
- Forensic Toxicology
- Forensic Psychology 
- Digital Forensic - analyzes, examines, identifies and recovers the digital evidences residing on electronic devices

## Digital Forensics
![[Pasted image 20260604203101.png]]
Involves,
- recovery
- preservation
- analysis
- presentation
of electronic data that has been stored transmitted or processed on digital devices such as computers, mobile phones and other digital storage media. 

Digital evidence: Information stored or transmitted in binary form that can be relied upon in court as evidence.
Ex:
	Device data
	Email and messaging data
	Internet data
	Social media data
	Cloud data
	Network data
	Audio and video data
	Metadata

> [!NOTE] Metadata
> Information about a file, such as the date and time it was created or modified, and can be used to piece together a timeline of events

Digital forensics emerged in response to computer related crime and the need to investigate digital evidence. 

Digital forensics has main categories, 
- Computer forensics
- Mobile forensics
- Network forensics

Technologies involved digital forensics, 
1. Imaging Technology
	Create forensic images of storage devices and analyze them using specialized software.
2. Data recovery tools
	Recover lost or deleted data from digital devices, even if the data has been intentionally deleted or hidden
3. Mobile Forensics
	Software and tools for mobiles to allow investigators to recover data from mobile devices and analyze.
4. Cloud Forensics
	To recover and analyze cloud based data
5. AI & ML
	Quickly identify patterns and anomalies in large datasets, making it easier for investigators to focus on areas of interest.
6. Blockchain Forensics
	Trace cryptocurrency transactions and analyze.
7. Live Forensics
	Analyze digital devices in real-time, allowing them to quickly identify and respond to security threats
8. Virtualization Technology
	Create virtual machines and environments for forensic analysis, reducing the need for physical hardware and making it easier to test and validate forensic tools and techniques

Digital forensics may used in two different scenarios, 
1. Criminal Law
	Evidence collected to support or oppose an argument in the court. 
	Similar to criminal investigations. 
2. Private Investigations
	Used by corporate world. 
	Used when companies are suspicious that employees may be performing an illegal activity.

## Digital forensics Procedures 
![[Pasted image 20260604213557.png]]
four phases,
1. Identification
2. Collection
3. Examination
4. Analysis

##### 1. Identification
1. Begins with gathering information from stakeholders like law enforcement, corporate security, legal teams. 
2. Review information to determine scope and objectives. 
3. Identify potential places, sources(devices, email accounts, clous storages) of digital evidence relevant to the case. 
Identify relevant metadata like
	Date and time
	Location of a file or piece of data
	User who created it
##### 2. Collection
1. Gather all potential sources.
2. Collect data using tools and techniques,
	Imaging a hard drive
	Creating forensics copy of a mobile device
	Network sniffing tools to capture network traffic. 

Should create a detailed chain of custody for each piece of evidence collected. 
Important to obtain a search warrant before collecting digital evidence. 

Chain of custody,
- A way to document the handling and movement of evidence in a digital forensics investigation. Its a record of who had control of the evidence. 
- The goal is to ensure that the evidence is reliable in court.
- It helps prevent tampering, contamination, or loss of evidence.
- Start when the evidence first collected and ends when presented in court. 
- Each person who handles the evidence must document their actions and sign the chain of custody form.
- Any break in the chain of custody can weaken the credibility and may result in exclude from the trial. 

Chain of custody records includes, 
- Date
- Time
- Location
- Description of the evidence
- Name of the person

##### 3. Examination
- Examine the evidence using software tools and manual analysis. 
- Purpose: determine the relevance and significance of digital evidence
- May also attempt to identify patterns or connections between different pieces of digital evidence.

##### 4. Analysis
- Reviews and interprets the digital evidence to draw conclusions and develop a report of findings. 
- Looks for patterns, connections, and other meaningful relationships to develop an understanding of the case
- Goal: provide a clear and detailed report of the digital evidence and findings that can be used in legal proceedings or other contexts

2. Technical skills
3. Passion for cyber security
4. Communication skills
